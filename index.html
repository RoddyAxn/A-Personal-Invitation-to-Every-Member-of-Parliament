Personal Letter to Every MP

Dear Member of Parliament,

I am writing to you personally because the next New Zealand Government does not necessarily have to be determined solely by the existing party blocs.

Every Member of Parliament ultimately holds an individual seat in the House of Representatives.

Some were elected directly by an electorate. Others entered Parliament through a party list.

Different constitutional and political routes brought us here, but once elected, every MP participates in determining one fundamental question:

WHO COMMANDS THE CONFIDENCE OF THE HOUSE?

I am asking you to consider a different possibility for the government that follows the 2026 General Election.

I, Karl Barkley, am prepared to put myself forward for election to Parliament and, if elected, to be considered as a prospective Prime Minister.

I am not asking you to abandon your political beliefs.

I am not asking you to resign from your party.

I am not asking you to promise how you will vote on every piece of legislation.

I am asking whether you would be prepared to consider supporting a government based on a wider parliamentary agreement in which MPs have substantially greater freedom to represent their electorates, principles and conscience.

COUNTRY BEFORE PARTY.
REPRESENTATION BEFORE DISCIPLINE.
CONFIDENCE WHERE NECESSARY.
INDEPENDENCE WHERE POSSIBLE.

If enough Members of Parliament are willing to discuss such an arrangement following the election, a new form of government becomes possible.

I invite you to read this package and return the enclosed response.

Yours sincerely,

Karl Barkley
Prospective Parliamentary Candidate

The Question Before Every MP

Parliament Chooses Who Can Govern

New Zealanders elect Members of Parliament.

Following an election, the political process establishes which person can lead a government capable of commanding the confidence of the House of Representatives.

The Governor-General then acts on the outcome of that political process.

That means government formation ultimately depends upon MPs.

Not every MP has to belong to the same political organisation.

Not every supporting MP has to agree with every government policy.

Governments can operate through coalition agreements, confidence-and-supply arrangements and other negotiated parliamentary relationships.

The Question

Following the 2026 General Election, would you be prepared to discuss supporting Karl Barkley as Prime Minister if:

1. Karl Barkley has been elected to Parliament;

2. sufficient MPs publicly indicate that they are prepared to support him on confidence; and

3. a workable agreement for stable government can be negotiated?

That is the proposition being placed before every MP.

What We Are Asking For

This package does not ask you to sign an unconditional promise.

Instead, MPs are offered four levels of response.

Option A — Confidence

I would be prepared, in principle, to support Karl Barkley as Prime Minister following the 2026 General Election if he is elected to Parliament and demonstrates sufficient parliamentary support to form a stable government.

Option B — Discussion

I am prepared to participate in discussions with Karl Barkley and other MPs about government formation, confidence and supply following the election.

Option C — Parliamentary Reform Only

I do not presently endorse Karl Barkley for Prime Minister, but I am interested in proposals that would provide MPs with greater independence from automatic party voting.

Option D — No

I do not wish to participate.

No MP should be required to pretend that they have made a decision they have not made. A genuine expression of interest is more valuable than an artificial pledge.

The Barkley Government Proposition

A GOVERNMENT BUILT AROUND CONFIDENCE, NOT COMPLETE UNIFORMITY

A Barkley-led government would seek agreement on a limited set of matters necessary to maintain stable government.

Confidence

Agreement concerning the circumstances in which participating MPs would support the Government on an explicit motion of confidence or no confidence.

Supply

Agreement concerning the passage of appropriations necessary for government to function.

Core Government Programme

A published programme containing the policy measures for which common parliamentary support has been negotiated.

Electorate Freedom

Outside matters expressly covered by the agreement, participating MPs should retain substantially greater freedom to vote according to their electorate mandate, their publicly stated principles, their conscience and the merits of individual legislation.

Transparency

Any agreement establishing or supporting the Government should be put in writing and made available to the public.

Seven Principles for a New Parliamentary Agreement

1. Parliament Is More Than Its Parties

Political parties are an important part of MMP, but Parliament consists of individual elected representatives.

2. Confidence Should Be Explicit

The question of who governs should be distinguishable from ordinary disagreements about individual bills.

3. Supply Provides Continuity

Government must be capable of obtaining the lawful authority required to fund public services.

4. MPs Should Have Room to Represent

Where an issue is not covered by an agreed governing programme, participating MPs should have greater ability to exercise independent judgment.

5. Agreements Should Be Public

The public should know what has been promised in exchange for parliamentary support.

6. Electorates Matter

Local representation should be an important component of parliamentary decision-making.

7. Stability and Independence Can Coexist

New Zealand should not have to choose between rigid party control and unstable government. A properly drafted confidence arrangement can provide both stability and greater parliamentary independence.

Questions an MP May Reasonably Ask

Can Karl Barkley become Prime Minister without being an MP?

No. Karl would need to be elected to Parliament before he could be appointed Prime Minister.

Are MPs directly electing a Prime Minister?

Not formally. The Governor-General appoints the Prime Minister after the political process establishes who can lead a government capable of commanding the confidence of the House.

Does signing this remove my party membership?

No. This package does not purport to determine an MP's membership of a political party. An MP considering a departure from an existing caucus position should separately consider their party rules, caucus obligations and applicable electoral law.

Am I promising to vote for Karl regardless of the election result?

No. The proposed statement is conditional upon Karl being elected and there being a credible parliamentary basis for a stable government.

Will I have to support every government bill?

That is not the proposed model. Any eventual agreement should specifically identify what constitutes confidence and supply and what other agreed policies, if any, participating MPs undertake to support.

Can MPs from different political traditions participate?

Yes, if they voluntarily find sufficient common ground. The object is not ideological uniformity.

What happens if Karl cannot command confidence?

Then he cannot constitutionally lead the Government.

Why contact every MP?

Because every MP should have the opportunity to consider the proposal personally rather than having the proposal filtered solely through party leadership.
